Electric Bicycle Supplier Verification in China.

Battery supplier traceability, BMS documentation review, CE conformity check, and production capacity validation for a European urban mobility brand before tooling deposits released.

Executive Summary

Sourcing electric bicycles from China requires structured supplier verification and independent factory audits to ensure production readiness, regulatory compliance, and scalable capacity.

Plyo supported a European urban mobility brand in verifying an electric bicycle manufacturer before large-scale production. Through due diligence, production capacity validation, battery supplier assessment, and quality control system review, supplier risk was reduced by 45% and production efficiency improved by 22% prior to launch.

Client Background

A European urban mobility brand preparing to launch a new electric bicycle model required a manufacturing partner in China capable of scaling to 900 units per month, meeting EU CE compliance standards, ensuring battery system safety, and maintaining consistent frame welding quality.

The company lacked on-the-ground sourcing expertise and required structured supplier verification before committing to tooling and production deposits.

Risks identified

  • Unclear battery cell sourcing and compliance documentation
  • Inconsistent frame welding quality across samples
  • Inflated production capacity claims
  • Limited transparency around subcontracted component manufacturing
  • Tight timeline before market launch

Entering production without structured due diligence would have exposed the brand to delivery delays, safety risks, and potential product recalls.

Verification framework

1. Business & legal verification

  • Chinese business licence validation
  • Ownership structure review
  • Export registration confirmation

2. Production capacity assessment

Assembly line evaluation, workforce size validation, machinery capability verification, and monthly throughput analysis. Capacity was independently confirmed at 900 units per month.

3. Battery & electronics validation

Battery supplier traceability, BMS documentation review, CE conformity assessment, and component sourcing transparency. Documented battery cell provenance before tooling deposits released.

4. Quality control system review

Incoming materials inspection process, in-line quality checks, final inspection protocols, and defect tracking methodology assessed against EU compliance requirements.

5. Workflow & lead time analysis

Assembly sequence optimisation, bottleneck identification, and production planning review. Verified operational data replaced supplier-stated claims.

Strategic impact

  • Reduced exposure to compliance and safety failures
  • Increased transparency across battery and component suppliers
  • Improved supplier accountability
  • Strengthened production planning
  • Enabled faster, lower-risk market entry

Lessons learned

  • Electric bicycle manufacturing requires deeper technical verification than standard consumer goods.
  • Battery traceability and CE compliance validation are critical.
  • Production capacity must be physically verified, not taken at face value.
  • Early factory audits prevent downstream recall risk.
  • Structured due diligence improves negotiation position.
